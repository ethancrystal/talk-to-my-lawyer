# I Sent a Demand Letter and Got No Response — What Now in California?

**Short answer: No response after your deadline means the recipient chose not to comply voluntarily. Your next moves are: (1) send a final notice stating you'll file suit in 10 days, (2) file in small claims court for claims up to $12,500, or (3) retain an attorney for larger amounts. The demand letter still did its job — it created the paper trail judges want to see.**

Silence feels like failure. It isn't. About 30-40% of demand letters receive no formal response, even when the recipient intends to eventually pay. Some people need the second shoe to drop. Others need to see the actual court summons. Your letter moved the dispute from "vague unhappiness" to "documented legal claim with a deadline." That's progress.

## Confirm They Actually Received It

Before escalating, verify delivery. If you sent via certified mail (USPS Form 3811), check tracking. Under CCP § 1013, certified mail is deemed received 5 days after mailing in California. If the letter was returned unclaimed, you have a service problem, not a response problem — find their current address and re-send.

If you sent via email only, you have no proof of receipt. Consider re-sending via certified mail before escalating.

## Your Three Options After Silence

**Option 1: Send a "Final Notice Before Filing" letter (adds 10-14 days)**

A second, shorter letter that says: "I sent a demand letter on [date]. You did not respond by the deadline. I intend to file a complaint in [Small Claims Court / Superior Court] within 10 days unless this matter is resolved." This converts roughly 10-15% of holdouts. The word "filing" hits different than "demand."


**Option 2: File in small claims court (adds 4-8 weeks to resolution)**

For claims up to $12,500 (individuals) or $6,250 (businesses) under CCP § 116.220-116.221, small claims is the fastest and cheapest court option. Filing costs $75-$100. No attorney required or even permitted. Your demand letter becomes Exhibit A — proof you tried good faith resolution first. California judges consistently view prior demand letters favorably under CCP § 128.7.

**Option 3: Hire an attorney for a civil suit (claims over $12,500)**

For larger amounts, or when you need discovery (subpoenas, depositions) to prove your case, an attorney files in Superior Court. This is slower (6-18 months) and more expensive, but the demand letter still serves as evidence of your pre-suit good faith.

> **California CCP § 116.220** — Small claims jurisdiction covers up to $12,500 for natural persons and up to $6,250 for businesses, corporations, and other entities per calendar year. Your unresponded demand letter is exactly the kind of pre-filing effort the court wants to see.

## When Silence Actually Means "I Can't Pay"

One reality check: some people don't respond because they're judgment-proof — no assets, no steady income, no bank account worth levying. A court judgment against a judgment-proof defendant is a piece of paper. It's enforceable for 10 years in California (CCP § 683.020) and renewable for another 10, so circumstances can change. But if you know the person is broke, consider whether the filing fee is worth it right now.

The demand letter still wasn't wasted. It established the claim date, documented the amount, and started any statute of limitations arguments in your favor.

Your [flat-fee demand letter](/blog/how-long-demand-letter-take-to-work-california) was the first step. If it didn't produce voluntary payment, the second step is filing — and the first step made the second one stronger.
