# What Is the Statute of Limitations for Suing Someone in California?

**Short answer: It depends on the type of claim. Written contracts give you 4 years (CCP § 337). Oral contracts give you 2 years (CCP § 339). Personal injury is 2 years (CCP § 335.1). Property damage is 3 years (CCP § 338). Miss the deadline and the court dismisses your case — no matter how valid your claim.**

The statute of limitations is a hard wall. Walk up to it one day late and your claim evaporates. Courts don't grant extensions because you were busy, didn't know the deadline existed, or spent months trying to negotiate. The clock runs whether you're paying attention or not.

## Every Common Deadline in California

| Claim Type | Deadline | Statute |
|------------|----------|---------|
| Written contract (breach) | 4 years | CCP § 337 |
| Oral contract (breach) | 2 years | CCP § 339 |
| Personal injury | 2 years | CCP § 335.1 |
| Property damage | 3 years | CCP § 338(b) |
| Fraud | 3 years from discovery | CCP § 338(d) |
| Professional malpractice (legal) | 1 year from discovery, max 4 years | CCP § 340.6 |
| Medical malpractice | 1 year from discovery, max 3 years | CCP § 340.5 |
| Defamation / libel / slander | 1 year | CCP § 340(c) |
| Unpaid wages | 3 years | Labor Code § 203 + CCP § 338 |
| Security deposit (landlord-tenant) | 2 years (oral lease) / 4 years (written) | CCP § 337/339 |
| Construction defects | 4 years from substantial completion | CCP § 337.1 |
| Debt on a credit card | 4 years (written) | CCP § 337 |


## When the Clock Starts

For most claims, the statute of limitations starts running on the date the harm occurred — the day they breached the contract, the day you were injured, the day the property was damaged.

Two exceptions matter:

**Discovery rule:** For fraud, malpractice, and latent defects, the clock starts when you discovered (or reasonably should have discovered) the harm. CCP § 338(d) gives you 3 years from discovery for fraud — not 3 years from when the fraud happened.

**Continuous accrual:** For ongoing wrongs (like each missed rent payment), each breach starts its own limitations period. If a tenant owes 6 months of back rent, each month's debt has its own 2-year or 4-year clock.

## Does a Demand Letter Stop the Clock?

No. Sending a demand letter does not toll, pause, or extend any statute of limitations in California. This is a common and dangerous misconception. People spend months negotiating via demand letters, believing they have unlimited time to file suit. They don't.

If your written contract was breached on June 1, 2022, you have until June 1, 2026 to file — regardless of how many demand letters you sent in between. Negotiate, but file before the deadline.

> **California CCP § 337** — An action upon any obligation or liability founded upon an instrument in writing must be commenced within 4 years. No provision in California law tolls this period for pre-litigation demand or negotiation.

## The Practical Takeaway

If you're approaching a limitations deadline, send your demand letter now and file simultaneously if needed. You can always dismiss the case if the demand letter produces a settlement. But you cannot un-expire a statute of limitations.
