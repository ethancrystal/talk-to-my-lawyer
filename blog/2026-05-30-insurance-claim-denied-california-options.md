# My Insurance Claim Was Denied in California — What Are My Options?

**Short Answer:** A denied claim is not the end. In California you can demand a written explanation, appeal internally, file a complaint with the Department of Insurance, and — if the denial was unreasonable — send a bad-faith demand letter or sue. Insurers owe you a duty of good faith.

When an insurer denies a claim you believe is covered, it's easy to assume you're stuck. You're not. California law imposes real obligations on insurers, and a denial that ignores those obligations can expose the company to penalties well beyond the original claim amount.

## What should I do first after a denial?

Start by getting the denial in writing with the specific reason and the policy language relied on. California's Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations (10 Cal. Code Regs. § 2695 et seq.) require insurers to explain claim denials in writing and reference the basis. Then re-read your policy's coverage and exclusions sections. Many denials rest on a misreading of facts or an exclusion that doesn't actually apply.

## Can I appeal the denial?

Yes. Almost every insurer has an internal appeal or reconsideration process. Submit a written appeal that addresses the stated reason directly and attaches supporting documentation — photos, estimates, medical records, receipts, or expert opinions. A well-documented appeal correcting a factual error often reverses the denial without any outside help.

## What is insurer "bad faith" in California?

California recognizes a legal duty called the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. When an insurer unreasonably denies or delays a valid claim, it can be liable for "bad faith" — and Insurance Code § 790.03 lists unfair claims practices like misrepresenting policy terms and failing to investigate promptly. A bad-faith claim can recover not just the policy benefits but also additional damages, and in egregious cases, punitive damages. That exposure is precisely why a credible demand letter can move an insurer.

## How does a demand letter help?

A demand letter to the insurer lays out the coverage, documents why the denial was wrong, cites the unfair-practices regulations, and demands payment by a deadline. It signals you understand your rights and are prepared to escalate. Insurers frequently re-evaluate once they see a letter framing the denial as potential bad faith. For the mechanics of building one, see our [complete guide to writing a demand letter to an insurance company in California](/blog/2026-05-30-how-to-write-demand-letter-to-insurance-company-california).

## Can I file a complaint with the state?

Yes. The California Department of Insurance (CDI) accepts consumer complaints and can investigate the insurer's handling of your claim. A CDI complaint doesn't force payment by itself, but it creates a regulatory record and sometimes prompts the insurer to reconsider.

## How long do I have to act?

Deadlines depend on your policy and claim type. Some property policies contain a contractual limitations period as short as one year for filing suit, while breach-of-contract claims often run longer. Because these windows can be short and are easy to miss, confirm your deadline early.
